## Voice

Third person limited, POV character named in the frontmatter (or your novel's
actual POV convention — first person, multiple simultaneous POVs, etc. Adjust
this whole section if your novel isn't third-limited). Never leave that head;
no observation the POV character couldn't make.

**If your novel uses a strict opening convention (e.g. the chapter, and every
scene within it, must open with the POV character's name as the literal first
word or opening clause) state it here explicitly as a firm house rule, not a
stylistic nudge** — this is the single most commonly broken rule in practice,
so it earns being spelled out even if it feels obvious. It matters most right
after a scene break, where the POV may have just shifted.

- No clichés. If a phrase feels available, it's worn.
- Varied sentence length; short sentences under pressure.

  Cut lines that only fill air.

  action over interior summary. (Adjust this if your novel is deliberately

  be distinguishable on **rhythm**, not just content — if two characters'
  sentences have the same architecture, the voice work hasn't happened.

## Structural execution

- Treat `Outline.md`'s `## Sanderson pass` as the chapter's structural
  contract. Make its progress signpost visible in prose; a beat code in
  frontmatter is not evidence the reader experienced a change.
- Let climactic solutions recombine established tools. Do not introduce a new
  power, mineral, rule, or item at the point where it solves the problem.
- Enforce limitations and current resource state at the moment of payoff: a
  cost must be earned before it is spent, equipment must be ready, resources
  must cost what they're established to cost, injuries must constrain action,
  and knowledge must have been acquired.
- End scenes and chapters in a meaningfully changed state unless a deliberately
  quiet beat is fulfilling a clear relationship, tone, or atmosphere promise.

## Continuity habits worth building

- The outline is a prediction; the prose is the fact. Update the chapter
  frontmatter's `characters:` and `locations:` to who and what actually appears.
- Check the opening against the **previous chapter's final lines**, physically.
  A chapter that continues from a cliffhanger inherits every detail of the
  situation — including small physical facts established moments earlier.
- Any number you introduce in a revision (counts, quantities, resources spent)
  has to be checked against what's actually on the page.
