# Continuity Checking Best Practices

Based on research into fiction proofreading and continuity management, here are the key categories to check.

## Character Consistency (Highest Priority)

**Physical/Appearance Traits**
- Hair color, length, style
- Scars, tattoos, distinguishing marks
- Height, build, clothing
- Injuries or physical disabilities
- Aging (time progression)

Check: Does the character look the same across chapters? If they get injured or cut their hair, is it mentioned explicitly?

**Personality & Voice**
- Speech patterns and dialect
- Emotional baseline (is an aggressive character suddenly gentle?)
- Phobias, habits, recurring behaviors
- Knowledge level (what they know at this point in the story)

Check: Does the character sound right? Would they know what they're acting on?

**Abilities & Equipment**
- Powers, skills, special training
- Weapons, tools, gear
- Skill levels (amateur vs. expert)
- Licenses or permissions (can they drive this ship?)

Check: Can they do what the scene asks? Did they learn this ability first?

**Status & Relationships**
- Health state (injured, healthy, exhausted)
- Squad membership or team assignment
- Relationships with other characters (do they know each other yet?)
- Social position or rank

Check: Can they be in this place? Should they know this other character?

## Location & World State (High Priority)

**Physical Description**
- Room layout, exits, windows
- Architecture and materials
- Furnishings and equipment
- Damage or recent changes

Check: Is the location described the same way? If it was destroyed, is it still destroyed?

**Occupants & Access**
- Who lives/works here
- Who's present right now
- Door locks, security systems
- Required permissions or keys

Check: Can the character access this? Who should be here?

**Tech & Systems State**
- Is the ship still damaged? Fixed? Upgraded?
- Do computers work? Are they online?
- Can you use the communications system?
- Power status (do lights work?)

Check: Has the state changed since the last mention? If so, was it explained?

**Travel & Distance**
- How far apart are locations?
- How fast can characters travel?
- How long does travel take?
- Roads, routes, obstacles

Check: Could they actually make this journey in the time allowed?

## Item & Prop Consistency (High Priority)

**Physical Description**
- Color, material, texture, size
- Distinguishing marks or damage
- How it's described the *first* time it's introduced

Check: Is the object described the same way every time it's mentioned? If a color or material term changes, is there an on-page reason (it was recolored, coated, damaged, revealed to be something else)?

**State & Ownership**
- Consumed, transformed, upgraded, damaged, lost
- Who's currently holding it or where it currently is
- Has a transfer of ownership/location happened on the page?

Check: Is the item being used in a state or location the manuscript hasn't actually shown it reaching yet?

**Watch for compound descriptors in the codex**
- A codex entry that joins two terms with a slash ("brass/bronze compass," "grey/green cloak") is not evidence the underlying chapters agree — it's often a sign an earlier pass noticed two different words used across chapters and folded them together instead of resolving which is correct (or deciding both are correct and updating the earlier chapters to match).
- When you see this pattern, open every chapter the bullet cites and compare the actual sentences, not just the codex's summary of them.

Check: Chapter 4 calls it "brass," Chapter 7 calls it "bronze" — does the codex's "brass/bronze" bullet mean this was actually reconciled in the prose, or just noticed and merged in the codex only?

## Cross-Artifact Fact Consistency (High Priority)

A chapter's prose, its `Summary.md`, and any codex mechanics page covering the
same event can each independently claim a quantitative or categorical fact —
and only the prose is authoritative. `Summary.md` is meant to be written *from*
the finished prose, but revision rounds routinely change a specific detail in
the manuscript (a number, a tag, a placement) without anyone regenerating the
Summary or updating the codex page that cited the old value. When that
happens, the Summary and codex still match each other, because both trace
back to the same stale intermediate draft — which makes a two-source
agreement look like confirmation instead of the drift it actually is.

**Facts of this shape need a three-way check — prose vs. Summary vs. codex,
prose wins ties:**
- Percentages and numeric readouts (a device's remaining charge, a stat value)
- Counts (supplies or ammunition remaining, opponents present, days elapsed)
- Rank, level, or classification tags (e.g. `Level 3, Uncommon` vs. `Level 3, Common`)
- Physical placement (which limb, which side, where exactly an effect landed)
- Which hand, arm, or side a piece of gear is used from

Check: does the chapter's own Summary state a percentage, count, or tag that
doesn't match what the actual chapter text at the cited line says? If so,
which one is the stale copy — and does a codex mechanics page repeat the
*same* stale value, making it look doubly confirmed?

This is a distinct failure mode from the compound-descriptor case above: there
the codex is quietly averaging two real disagreements; here, two artifacts
that *should* be identical (Summary and codex both meant to reflect the
manuscript) have silently diverged from the manuscript itself while staying
in sync with each other.

## Timeline & Sequence (High Priority)

**Event Order**
- Did events happen in the order described?
- Did cause-and-effect make sense?
- Did characters have enough time to react?

Check: Could the POV character actually learn about X before reacting to it if X just happened 5 pages ago?

**Story Time Progression**
- Days, weeks, months passing
- Seasonal changes
- Character aging
- Healing time for injuries

Check: If a character was injured 2 pages ago, are they healed too fast?

**Flashback & Revelation Timing**
- When did the reader learn important info?
- Is a character using knowledge they shouldn't have yet?
- Are clues revealed in the right order?

Check: Does this clue confirm a mystery too early? Did the reader already know this should be a secret?

## Plot Thread Management (Medium Priority)

**Open Threads from Prior Chapters**
- Are unresolved questions still unresolved?
- Do they need addressing in this chapter?
- Or are they intentionally saved for later?

Check: A question was raised at the end of Chapter 6 — is it addressed in this chapter, or is it an open thread for Chapter 7?

**Character Arc Progression**
- Is this chapter moving the character closer to their goal?
- Do their actions align with their known motivations?
- Are they making progress on their arc?

Check: the protagonist is set on uncovering who sent the letter — are they taking steps toward that goal?

**Subplot Status**
- Minor conflicts introduced earlier
- Secondary character arcs
- Recurring motifs or callbacks

Check: has a secondary character dropped out of the book without explanation? Is their subplot still moving forward?

## Mechanical & System Consistency (Medium Priority)

**Rule Adherence**
- Magic/tech system rules (leveling, stat limits, or other established mechanics, if your story has them)
- Combat mechanics (does the arena have rules?)
- Social systems (does the institution actually work this way?)

Check: if your world has a progression or magic system, does this chapter's gain obey its documented rate and cost?

**In-World Logic**
- How does the economy work?
- Do character actions have logical consequences?
- Are there unexplained miracles or coincidences?

Check: How did this character afford that equipment? Was it explained?

## Lore & World Consistency (Lower Priority, But Catches Deep Issues)

**Species Behavior & Culture**
- Do alien species act consistently with their biology?
- Are cultural norms respected?
- Do faction members behave like faction members?

Check: Would a member of this species/faction really do this? Does it violate what we know about their culture?

**Historical Consistency**
- Past events (does the backstory still hold?)
- Known history (did something in history class contradict this?)
- Relationships between factions (are they still enemies?)

Check: Chapter 2 said these factions are at peace — why are they fighting in Chapter 6?

**Magic/Tech Rules**
- Are established rules followed?
- Do inventions use consistent logic?
- Are limits respected (can't do the impossible unless you've explained it)

Check: The suit can't survive vacuum — why does it work in space in this chapter?

## What this checklist is NOT scoped to catch

This checklist (and the `continuity-reviewer` skill it backs) is deliberately
scoped to a single chapter plus the codex pages it references, for cost
reasons — see `review-loop.md`. Some real continuity problems only show up
when comparing files across the *whole* vault at once, and no per-chapter
pass will ever catch them:

- Beat-order contradictions between a Thread file's full beat list and what
  chapters have actually claimed, including chapters that are outlined but
  not yet drafted.
- Phase/status metadata drifting out of sync across `00 Index.md`, an arc's
  outline file, and the Thread status lines.
- Backlink orphans — a note that resolves fine but has zero inbound links.

These need a separate, periodic whole-vault audit (recommended cadence: every
arc boundary, or every 3–4 chapters), not a bigger per-chapter bundle.

## Quick Triage: What Fails the Chapter?

If you find an issue, ask:
1. **CRITICAL** — Does this break the plot or make no sense? Fix it.
2. **WARNING** — Does this contradict established lore? Fix it before publishing.
3. **NOTE** — Is this a polish issue or intentional? Mark for later or leave it.
