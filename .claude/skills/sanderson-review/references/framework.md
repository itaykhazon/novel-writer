## 1. Promise

A promise is a reader expectation created by emphasis, framing, repetition, genre convention, or an explicit goal. Review six promise types:

| Type | Question |
|---|---|
| Tone | What emotional and stylistic experience is this chapter/book offering? |
| Story | What eventual event, answer, confrontation, or change is being implied? |
| Character | What does the character want, fear, hide, or need to become? |
| Conflict | What prevents the desired outcome, and why will it not be easy? |
| Structure/genre | What familiar reading contract—mystery, progression, romance, heist, dungeon loop—is invoked? |
| Mechanic | Which tool, limitation, resource, clue, or rule is being taught for later use? |

### Promise failures

- **False promise:** emphasis trains the reader to expect a story the outline does not intend to deliver.
- **Invisible promise:** the outline assumes an expectation the prose never creates.
- **Dropped promise:** an important expectation receives no renewal for so long that progress becomes unreadable.
- **Competing promise:** a secondary thread receives stronger framing than the intended main thread.
- **Over-explicit promise:** the prose explains the destination so completely that no discovery remains.

Do not demand every promise pay off in the same chapter. Identify whether the chapter pays it, progresses it, renews it, deliberately redirects it, or leaves it untouched.

## 2. Progress

Progress is a satisfying change in the reader's sense of distance, cost, understanding, or possibility. Chronological motion alone is not progress.

Track these channels:

| Channel | Visible signposts |
|---|---|
| External goal | obstacle cleared, route chosen, resource gained/lost, objective revised |
| Information | clue found, hypothesis rejected, question sharpened, implication understood |
| Relationship | behavior changes, trust offered/refused, boundary renegotiated, ritual formed/broken |
| Internal belief | contradiction noticed, coping strategy fails, self-story weakens, choice changes |
| Capability/mastery | rule understood, combination improved, limitation mastered, cost willingly paid |
| Setting/system | institution reacts, world rule deepens, consequence of culture/mechanic becomes concrete |
| Tone | a promised comic, horrific, wondrous, or intimate experience is actually delivered |

### Progress failures

- **Motion without change:** travel, fighting, discussion, or explanation leaves all important states the same.
- **Repeated progress:** several scenes demonstrate the same relationship or capability level without escalation.
- **Invisible progress:** the state changes, but the reader receives no signpost.
- **Unearned leap:** competence, intimacy, insight, or certainty jumps past needed intermediate states.
- **Single-channel middle:** the chapter advances only logistics while its promised character/mystery thread stalls, or vice versa.

Progress may be negative. A worsening relationship, disproven theory, lost capability, or failed coping mechanism can be strong progress when it changes the state and points toward payoff.

## 3. Payoff

A payoff answers a promise. Strong payoffs are prepared, causal, costly enough for their scale, and matched to the story's emotional core.

Check:

1. **Recognition:** Can the reader connect the payoff to an earlier promise, rule, image, choice, or question?
2. **Preparation:** Were the necessary facts and capabilities planted before use?
3. **Causality:** Did character choices and prior progress produce the result?
4. **Escalation:** Did the route become difficult enough for the payoff to feel earned?
5. **Specificity:** Could this resolution occur only in this story with these characters and mechanics?
6. **Cost/limitation:** Do established constraints remain active at the moment of success?
7. **Consequence:** Does the payoff change the story rather than simply close a task?
8. **Surprise plus inevitability:** Is the exact form fresh while still making sense in retrospect?

### Payoff failures

- **Missing payoff:** the chapter's central expectation receives no answer or deliberate handoff.
- **Unprepared payoff:** the needed clue, rule, resource, relationship, or choice appears too late.
- **Mechanical payoff:** the event occurs, but the emotional or thematic function does not.
- **Double payoff:** two adjacent scenes deliver the same reveal or victory without distinct functions.
- **Wrong-scale payoff:** a minor setup receives climactic weight or a major promise receives a throwaway answer.
- **Costless choice:** the story labels a decision meaningful without a credible alternative or sacrifice.

## 4. Functional beat verification

Compare the manuscript to the outline by narrative function, not wording.

### Status definitions

**LANDED**

- The planned event or an equivalent occurs.
- Its intended character, plot, mystery, relationship, or mechanic function is legible.
- The chapter reaches the required resulting state.

**PARTIAL**

- Part of the event occurs, or the function is only gestured toward.
- The reader could miss the intended change, cost, realization, or causal link.
- A small insertion, escalation, reaction, or signpost could complete it without redesigning the scene.

**MISSING**

- No functional equivalent appears.
- The expected setup, turn, decision, reveal, or consequence is absent.
- Later beats depend on a state the manuscript has not created.

**ALTERED**

- The manuscript delivers a materially different event, cause, meaning, owner, order, or outcome.
- The change may be effective, but it changes the plan and needs a conscious outline/canon decision.

### Beat rules

- Do not count a name, object, or action as a landed beat when the outline requires a realization, relationship change, or choice.
- Do not penalize different staging when it delivers the same function and exit state.
- Mark a beat `PARTIAL`, not `MISSING`, when the manuscript establishes the event but lacks its intended emphasis or consequence.
- Record meaningful unplanned beats when they create new promises, consume a later payoff, alter canon, or change the chapter's exit state.
- Do not list ordinary connective tissue as an unplanned beat.

### Outline-only status definitions

Use these instead of prose-status labels when the work has not been drafted:

- **PLANNED:** The event, intended function, and resulting state are specified.
- **PARTIAL:** Some function is planned, but a necessary setup, change, cost,
  reaction, or consequence is missing.
- **ABSENT:** No planned unit provides the required function.
- **CONFLICT:** The plan contradicts canon, another beat, a resource state, or
  the required exit state.

These statuses judge the plan's causal completeness, not whether a beat has
landed for a reader. Execution remains unverified until prose exists.

## 5. Scene progression

For each scene record:

1. **Entry state:** the relevant goal, belief, relationship, information, capability, and pressure at entry.
2. **Immediate desire:** what the POV character wants during this scene.
3. **Resistance:** what blocks, complicates, or makes the desire costly.
4. **Choice/turn:** the decision, revelation, disaster, reversal, or changed behavior.
5. **Exit state:** what is now harder, clearer, closer, costlier, newly possible, or no longer possible.
6. **Forward pull:** the consequence or question that makes the next scene necessary.

A scene may function as a sequel/reaction rather than a confrontation. It still needs a changed understanding, dilemma, decision, relationship state, or tonal fulfillment.

### Scene warning signs

- Entry and exit states are materially identical.
- The scene repeats a prior proof of competence or affection.
- Characters discuss the plot but reach no new conclusion, choice, or disagreement.
- A major turn lands without reaction or consequence.
- The scene exists only to transmit codex information.
- The outline beat is present only in summary language, not dramatized experience.

## 6. Try-fail escalation

Use try-fail as a flexible causal ladder:

1. An obvious or habitual method fails.
2. A smarter or more committed method fails, succeeds at a cost, or exposes a deeper problem.
3. Success comes through changed understanding, accepted limitation, cooperation, sacrifice, or a recombination of established tools.

Not every sequence needs three attempts. The essential test is whether each attempt changes information, tactics, cost, or character. Repetition at the same difficulty is not escalation.

For mystery progress, substitute **hypothesis → contradiction → revised hypothesis**. For relationship progress, use **defense → challenged defense → changed behavior**. For internal arcs, use **coping mechanism → consequence → new choice**.

## 7. Earned mechanics and reveals

Apply Sanderson's magic-system diagnostics to technology, LitRPG rules, politics, clues, and character skills:

- A tool may solve in proportion to reader understanding.
- Limitations, weaknesses, and costs must remain active.
- Expand established implications before adding new capabilities.
- Soft/unknown forces should primarily complicate, create wonder, or create fear until understood.
- A reveal should reorganize known evidence, not replace missing evidence with explanation.

Flag a solution when the outline or prose spends an unearned resource, suppresses a known limitation, introduces a one-use ability, or relies on knowledge the POV/reader was not given.

Trace significant resources as a ledger: opening state → gain/loss → spend/strain → closing state. A resource can be physical, mechanical, informational, relational, political, or emotional. If a planned payoff depends on a resource whose state cannot be traced, treat the payoff as unverified.

## 8. Character movement

Use both character triangles from the working guide:

- **Motivation, personality, values:** what the character wants, how they habitually act, and what line or principle governs their decisions.
- **Proactive, relatable, capable:** whether their choices cause outcomes, their desires/costs are legible, and competence appears in specific behavior.

A planned arc needs movement in at least one relevant state: desire, belief, relationship, capability, limitation, status, or willingness to pay a cost. Plot happening near a character is not character progress.

## 9. Worldbuilding and progression

- Prefer expanding an established element's consequences and combinations before adding another noun or power.
- A major mechanic should affect plot options, character choices, and the lived setting.
- Ground unfamiliar abstractions in a concrete POV need, action, comparison, or consequence before layering terminology.
- Reveal the portion of the iceberg required for the current decision; codex depth is not a mandate to explain it all.
- LitRPG progression should change tactics, identity, pressure, options, or costs. A number rising without a changed decision is weak progress.

## 10. Severity thresholds

### BLOCKER

Use when the plan cannot earn its required climax or exit state without an author decision, missing setup, unavailable resource, unestablished capability, or contradiction in the causal chain. Do not invent the decision to clear the blocker.

### IMPORTANT

Use when one or more are true:

- an expected beat is `MISSING`, materially `ALTERED`, `ABSENT`, or materially `CONFLICT`;
- the chapter does not reach its required exit state;
- its central promise is false, dropped, or unanswered without a deliberate handoff;
- progress is absent across a substantial scene or sequence;
- a major solution, twist, or emotional turn is unearned;
- a mechanic violates its established cost or limitation at payoff;
- an unplanned beat consumes or contradicts a later outlined payoff.

### OPPORTUNITY / SUGGESTION

Use when the function exists but could become clearer or stronger through:

- better signposting;
- a visible intermediate step;
- sharper escalation;
- a reaction/consequence beat;
- earlier setup;
- more specific character choice;
- stronger thematic or mechanic interconnection.

Do not inflate a preference into `IMPORTANT`.

## 11. Boundaries with other reviewers

- Flag pacing only when rate or missing space prevents progress/payoff from registering; otherwise route to `pacing-review`.
- Flag continuity only when a contradiction invalidates a beat or solution; otherwise route to `continuity-reviewer`.
- Draft enough replacement prose to make a structural fix actionable, but do not line-edit the chapter; route sentence craft to `prose-review`.
- Mention grammar only if it makes the beat's meaning genuinely ambiguous; otherwise route to `proofread`.
